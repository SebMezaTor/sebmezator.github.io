Little Embers Café: designing a calmer way to browse a coffee menu

A five-page, fully responsive café website built solo with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript - solving a real usability problem (cluttered, image-heavy café menus) for a fictional coffee shop.

The Problem

Most café customers discover a shop on social media before they ever walk in, but the websites they land on work against that. Menus are buried in photo carousels instead of a structured text, and there's no fast way to answer "what do they have, and what does it cost?"

The brief: design a coffee shop website that treats the menu as the core product, organized, scannable, and easy to filter, while still feeling warm and handcrafted rather than transactional.

Why This Concept

Little Embers was chosen over two other concepts because it paired a real usability problem with room to demonstrate responsive layout, JavaScript interaction, and a full multi-page navigation system plus a flexible scope that could absorb an optional gift card page without derailing the timeline.

Research & Planning

Since Little Embers isn't a real café, I couldn't interview actual customers, so I leaned on secondary research instead: studying real café sites like Tierra Mia Coffee, reading small-business owners debate whether a website is even worth it on Reddit, and pulling structural guidance from industry write-ups on what makes a coffee shop site convert.

That research shaped a two-milestone plan: the first two weeks for scope, UX research, and visual direction; the following three weeks for building and testing the core pages. The gift card page was flagged early as a cut candidate if time ran short, it ended up making the final build.

Design Process

I wireframed all four core pages at medium fidelity before touching color or type, then explored two full moodboard directions before committing to one. Here's both, side by side.
