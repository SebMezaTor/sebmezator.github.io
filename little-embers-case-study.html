Case Study - Front-End Web Development

Little Embers Café: designing a calmer way to browse a coffee menu

A five-page, fully responsive café website built solo with vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript - solving a real usability problem (cluttered, image-heavy café menus) for a fictional Tempe coffee shop.

01

The Problem

Most café customers discover a shop on social media before they ever walk in - but the websites they land on work against that. Menus are buried in photo carousels instead of structured text, prices are hard to scan, and there's no fast way to answer "what do they have, and what does it cost?"

The brief: design a coffee shop website that treats the menu as the core product - organized, scannable, and easy to filter - while still feeling warm and handcrafted rather than transactional.

Why this concept

Little Embers was chosen over two concepts because it paired a real usability problem with room to demonstrate responsive layout, JS interaction, and a full multi-page navigation system - plus a flexible scope that could absorb an optional gift card page without derailing the timeline.

02

Resarch & Direction

Research drew from real café sites (Tierra Mia Coffee), small business owner discussions on Reddit, and industry guidance on coffee shop web design - used to ground the fictional brand in real usability patterns rather than pure aesthetics.

Concept A - "A Gentle Start" Selected

Warm-espresso palette, calm morning-coffee imagery, Playfair Display headings with Lato body text. Chosen for its cozy, handcrafted feel.
