Case Study - Front-End Web Development

Little Embers Café: designing a calmer way to browse a coffee menu

A five-page, fully responsive café website built solo with vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript - solving a real usability problem (cluttered, image-heavy café menus) for a fictional Tempe coffee shop.

01

The Problem

Most café customers discover a shop on social media before they ever walk in - but the websites they land on work against that. Menus are buried in photo carousels instead of structured text, prices are hard to scan, and there's no fast way to answer "what do they have, and what does it cost?"

The brief: design a coffee shop website that treats the menu as the core product - organized, scannable, and easy to filter - while still feeling warm and handcrafted rather than transactional.

Why this concept

Little Embers was chosen over two concepts because it paired a real usability problem with room to demonstrate responsive layout, JS interaction, and a full multi-page navigation system - plus a flexible scope that could absorb an optional gift card page without derailing the timeline.

02

Resarch & Direction

Research drew from real café sites (Tierra Mia Coffee), small business owner discussions on Reddit, and industry guidance on coffee shop web design - used to ground the fictional brand in real usability patterns rather than pure aesthetics.

Concept A - "A Gentle Start" Selected

Warm-espresso palette, calm morning-coffee imagery, Playfair Display headings with Lato body text. Chosen for its cozy, handcrafted feel.

03

From Wireframe to Interface

Medium-fidelity wireframe layout and hierarchy for all four core pages before any visual styling began - including the menu page's category filter system (All / Hot Coffee / Cold Coffee / Non-Coffee / Pastries / Seasonal) that became the site's core interaction.

One Change from Feedback

The original homepage wireframe showed prices on the featured drink cards. A peer review flagged that this made the homepage feel transactional rather than welcoming - prices were moved exclusively to the Menu page, and the homepage now sells the experience, not the price tag.

04

Building in Three Stages

Stage 1 - Structure & Foundation

Semantic HTML across all five pages, with CSS custom properties for color, type, spacing, and shadow set up first so every later decision had a single source of truth.

Stage 2 - Interactivity & Responsiveness

JavaScript-powered menu category filtering, hamburger navigation, dark mode toggle with localStorage persistence, and mobile-first breakpoints at 601px and 901px.

Stage 3 - Refinement & Polish

Real photography, Font Awesome icons, hover animation, the gift card live preview, and contact form validation - turning a functional skeleton into a finished product.

05

What broke, and how it got fixed

Building solo across five pages at once surfaced bugs later than ideal - a lesson in incremental testing carried into later debugging.

-

Gift Card Live Preview

Color swatch and custom message inputs weren't updating the preview in real tim, and the success state after purchase wasn't displaying - required extensive event-binding debugging.

-

Hamburger Navigation

Nav links failed to display consistently across all five pages; ultimately fixed with a min-height property on the mobile nav container.

-

Dark Mode + NAV Layout

Toggle and theme persistence worked correctly, but enabling dark mode shifted nav links left - a layout conflict not fully resolved within the project timeline.

06

Peer Testing & Refinement

With no real customer base to test against (the café is fictional), two classmates reviewed the live site as stand-ins for the target user during a peer review.

"The layout felt calm and welcoming, and the testimonials section in particular felt genuine and authentic." Peer Reviewer 1
"The color palette and typography were well suited to the café concept - the menu page's category transitions were a standout." Peer Reviewer 2

Changed Made From That Feedback

Centered text beneath images in the featured drinks and testimonials sections for visual consistency, and corrected the menu page's content alignment so it shared the same horizontal axis as the hero section above it - the most visually noticeable fix of the two.

Shipping a multi-page site with zero frameworks sharpened real debugging instincts, and reinforced the value of testing one page fully before moving to the next.
