Case Study - Front-End Web Development

Little Embers Cafe: designing a calmer way to browse a coffee menu

A five page, fully responsive café website built solo with vanilla HTML, CSS, and JavaScript - solving a real usability problem (cluttered, image-heavy café menus) for a fictional Tempe coffee shop.

The Problem

Most café customers discover a shop on social media before they ever walk in, but the websites they land on work against that. Menus are buried in photo carousels instead of a structured text, prices are hard to scan, and there's no fast way to answer "what do they have, and what does it cost?"

The brief: design a coffee shop website that treats the menu as the core product - organized, scannable, and easy to filter, while still feeling warm and handcrafted rather than transactional.

Why This Concept

Little Embers was chosen over two other concepts because it paired a real usability problem with room to demonstrate responsive layout, JS interaction, and a full multi-page navigation system, plus a flexible scope that could absorb an optional gift card page without derailing the timeline.

02

Research & Direction

Research drew from real café sites (Tierra Mia Coffee), small-business owner discussions on Reddit, and industry guidance on coffee shop web design - used to ground the fictional brand in real usability patterns rather than pure aesthetics.
