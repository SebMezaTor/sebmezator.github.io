Case Study - Front-End Development

Saga V: coding the ritual behind a tarot-inspired mocktail brand

A 13-person agile team built a full e-commerce brand site for Saga V, a non-alcoholic mocktail line packaged as collectible tarot cards. As part of the web development team, I translated the UI/UX team's Figma wireframes into the live About  Us page, brand story and milestone timeline.

The Fool - Beginning

The Brief

Saga V is a mocktail brand built around a simple idea: every flavor is paired with its own tarot card, turning each drink into a small collectible ritual rather than just a beverage. The creative brief asked our team to bring the product line and its brand storytelling to life online.

As the web development team, our job was to give the brand a user-friendly e-commerce platform for visibility and customer acquisition, coding fully-functional, responsive prototypes from the UI/UX team's Figma designs, and matching the digital identity to Saga V's high-end packaging and storytelling vision.

Scope note -> this case study covers the About Us page specifically, since that's the place I built end-to-end. The rest of the site (home, recipe blog, shop, cart, checkout) was split across the rest of the web dev team from the same design system.

The Magician - Process

II
The High Priestess - Direction

Narrowing Three Concepts to One

Every team member took part in early concept brainstorming, and three ideas stood out enough to develop into sketches. Cross-team feedback on how each would fit the broader project cut two of them:

Velvet Hour

Conflicted with the Saga V tarot theme the brand team had already committed to.

Build-Your-Own Recipe

A custom mocktail recipe builder conflicted with the brand team's fixed drink selection.

Product Detail Modal

The concept we carried forward, a focused product quick-view used across the shop experience.

Design direction as a whole was led by the brand team, which kept the dev team's job clear: build what design signed off on, flag what didn't translate to the browser, and stay out of decisions that weren't ours to make.

III
The Empress - Craft

From Wireframe to Code: About Us

The About Us wireframe laid out three moments: a hero, an "Our Story" block pairing text with a photo, and a milestone timeline tracing Saga V's history. My own notes from the project describe the intent behind it:

"The concept of the about us page shows the user how the brand was started (our story section), the evolution through the different milestones the brand was known for following the year, and the ability o connect with customers through a newsletter sign up."

The core design idea was a clean narrative hierarchy - establishing who the company is, why it exists, what it has done, and how users can join in by signing up for the newsletter. Building that meant working mobile-first in code just as we had mobile and desktop wireframes side by side in Figma:

  • Flex layout for the hero and story section:I used CSS flexbox to organize the "Our Story" hero image and text block so their proportions held up from a stacked mobile view up through wider breakpoints.
  • Flex layout for the footer:the same approach organized the footer links to match the wireframes column structure across screen sizes.
  • Milestone timeline:the trickiest piece: a connected, shape-based timeline of three milestone cards (Inspiration 2020 -> Initial Concept 2023 -> Saga V Is Born 2025). I hadn't finished the connecting-line geometry by the time of our team presentation, and finished it as I continued building out the page afterward.
IV
The Hierophant - Fit

Why This page, For This Brief

The About Us Page tied directly back to the creative brief: it showcased the dev team's ability to use auto-layout and responsive design, and carried the project through its required low, mid, and high fidelity deliverables. It also depended on other teams' work to come together pairing header imagery from the content team's visual storytelling with a tone of voice set by the brand team.

V
The Tower - Friction

Challenges & Turning Points

Running a 13-person team on one site surfaced problems that don't show up on a solo project:

  • Large group coordination across design and dev
  • Schedule conflicts between team members
  • Limited updates when a page was in progress between standups
  • Cross-collaboration between the brand, content, and dev teams staying aligned on one vision.

The turning point was concept and content delivery once the brand team's direction and content were locked in, the dev team had a stable target to build against instead of designing against a moving one.

VI
The Star - Feedback

What I was Checking For

Going into review, the feedback I most wanted was about readability and flow - whether the page's pacing kept users oriented as they scrolled, rather than losing them between the story section and milestone timeline. That framing shaped the spacing and hierarchy decisions across the page.

VII
The Sun - Outcome

Outcome & What It Taught Me

As a team, we shipped the full funnel from wireframe to live pages - landing page, About, recipe blog, product quick-view, shop, and checkout - standardizing cards, spacing, and type scale along the way, with attention to accessibility and performance. That meant staying integrated with the brand team on palette and component tokens, the content team on copy and SEO, and the packaging team on sustainability and traceability cues.

Saga V taught me how to be one accurate link in a chain. Matching someone else's design system precisely - spacing, hierarchy, tone, is a different discipline than making those calls myself on solo projects, it is much closer to what front-end development looks like on a real team of this size.

Learning to reach for flex layouts for both the hero and the footer, and working through shaped-based geometry for the milestone timeline, pushed my CSS further than a solo project would have - mostly because i couldn't quietly redesign around a piece I found difficult. It had to match the wireframe.
